# A.2.5 Tech team meeting agenda

## Summary

The tech team works in two-week sprints, so it can ship and learn every two weeks. Each sprint has six meetings: it opens with sprint planning, checks progress midway, and closes with a demo of everything shipped. Governance, one-to-one feedback, and an all-hands update keep roles, people, and priorities in step.

| Week | Day | Meeting | Purpose |

The tactical and governance meetings are the tech team's versions of the general formats in [A.2.3 Tactical meeting agenda](tactical-meeting-agenda.md) and [A.2.4 Governance meeting agenda](governance-meeting-agenda.md). The steps below are the tech team's own.

## Tactical 1: Sprint planning {#tactical-sprint-planning}

**Week 1, Monday.** Agree what the team will deliver in this sprint, sized to the capacity it actually has.

1. **Review top backlog priorities.** Start from the most important items.
2. **Engineers propose S/M/L estimates.** The people doing the work size each item as small, medium, or large.
3. **Choose sprint commitments based on capacity.** Commit only to what fits the time available.
4. **Define "done" for each item.** Agree what a finished item looks like before work starts.
5. **Confirm owners, dependencies, and stretch work.** Each item leaves with one owner, its known dependencies, and any stretch work to pull in if time allows.

## Governance 1: Roles and responsibilities {#governance}

**Week 1, Wednesday.** Keep roles, domains, and accountabilities current as the work changes. For why we work through explicit roles, see [1.4 Structure](../../dna/structure.md#roles-circles-decision-rights).

1. **Surface governance tensions.** Name places where responsibility is unclear, missing, or overlapping.
2. **Clarify roles, domains, and accountabilities.** Agree who owns what.
3. **Adjust recurring responsibilities or policies.** Change the standing arrangements the tension points to.
4. **Record changes and close.** Update the governance record so everyone works from the same picture.

## 1:1 {#one-on-one}

**Week 1, Friday.** A private conversation about how the work is going for the person, not only what they shipped. For general guidance on running one-to-ones, see [3.4.4 Choosing the right meeting](../../leadership/facilitation.md#choosing-the-right-meeting); for giving useful feedback, see [3.1.1 Feedback culture](../../leadership/culture-in-leadership.md#feedback-culture).

1. **Check workload and capacity.** Is the current load sustainable?
2. **Give and receive feedback.** Feedback runs in both directions.
3. **Discuss work style, blockers, and support needed.** Surface what is getting in the way, and what would help.
4. **Agree on any personal or process adjustments.** Leave with specific changes, if any are needed.

## Tactical 2: Sprint check {#sprint-check}

**Week 2, Monday.** A midpoint check, so problems surface while there is still time to act on them.

1. **Review each committed outcome.** Go through every sprint commitment.
2. **Mark on track, at risk, or blocked.** Give each one a clear status.
3. **Resolve blockers.** Use the meeting to unblock work, not just to list what's stuck.
4. **Re-scope if necessary.** Reduce or defer work rather than letting the deadline drift silently.
5. **Pull stretch work only if capacity exists.** Committed work comes first.

## All-hands {#all-hands}

**Week 2, Wednesday.** Make sure everyone shares the same picture of where the organization is going.

1. **Rohan gives organization updates.**
2. **Explain major strategic changes and priorities.** Say what changed and why.
3. **Share wins, risks, and upcoming events.**
4. **Open Q&A and discussion.**

## Demo day {#demo-day}

**Week 2, Friday.** Close the sprint by showing the work and learning from it.

1. **Demo everything shipped.** Show the working results.
2. **Compare committed vs. completed.** Be honest about the gap.
3. **Note what worked and what didn't.**
4. **Capture lessons for the next sprint.**
5. **Update the backlog.** The updated backlog feeds the next sprint planning meeting.

## Meeting record {#meeting-record}

Keep each meeting's notes with the team's current work records; this page holds the repeatable format. Record decisions and their reasons where the people doing the work can find them.

| Item | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Date and participants | Who met and when |
| Outcome or question | What the group needed to advance |
| Evidence and learning | Links to work, demo results, and relevant observations |
| Decision and reason | What was decided, by whom, and why |
| Open question or blocker | What remains unresolved and who will follow up |
| Next action | Owner and agreed completion or follow-up date |

For the strategy behind the tech team's work, see [E.4.1 Tech team: strategy primer](../staff/department-specific-guidance.md#tech-team-strategy). For general facilitation and update practices, see [3.4 Facilitation](../../leadership/facilitation.md).
