# E.4 Department-specific guidance

## Summary

The tech team builds systems that help people become supporters, members, and organizers. The team favors scalable systems, information that AI can work with, and learning through frequent prototypes, and it works in two-week sprints.

## E.4.1 Tech team: strategy primer {#tech-team-strategy}

**Users and outcomes.** Start with the people a system serves and the next step it helps them take. We practice “big organizing”: volunteers recruit more volunteers, increasing the movement's capacity to act. For the tech team, that means improving the path from an action to further participation and responsibility. Exponential growth is an ambition, not a measured result or a guaranteed growth rate.

The [movement flywheel in 1.2.5](../../dna/strategy.md#how-the-cycle-compounds) shows the act, recruit, train cycle and the systems that could support each stage, such as a member portal, dues, Discord, courses, and a phone app. It does not establish that each system or program is available or adopted.

**Prioritization and technology choices.** Three principles guide the team:

- **Scalability even over speed.** Consider how a system would serve thousands of new people each month. Treat that scale as a design scenario rather than a forecast.
- **AI-native over familiarity.** Choose systems whose information is machine-readable and whose work can be captured and understood by AI.
- **Prototype over perfection.** Iterate and deploy every two weeks to learn from actual use. The [two-week sprint cycle](#tech-team-meeting-format) puts this into practice.

These principles require judgment together. A prototype should answer a useful question, and a system choice should help the movement achieve an outcome. Record the reason for consequential tradeoffs so other contributors can understand the decision.

**Shared information: the company brain.** We aim to build a “company brain” with access to as much information about the movement as possible. Its purpose is to connect activity, metrics, insights, and strategy so AI can suggest next steps and people can focus on work requiring human judgment.

![Company brain: activity from supporters, members, and organizers feeds metrics, which inform insights and strategy.](/diagrams/company-brain.svg)

[Open the company brain diagram at full size](/diagrams/company-brain.svg).

In text: supporter signals include social-media views, website clicks, email engagement, and event attendance. Member signals include account creation, membership sign-ups, Discord activity, and course completion. Organizer signals include actions, events, meetings, and updated governance records. These feed metrics about what happened, insights about what was learned, and strategy about what to do next. They are candidate signals, not confirmed data integrations or performance measures.

The implementation, ownership, information-access rules, and human decision process for the company brain remain unresolved. The aim to capture information does not by itself authorize access to personal or restricted information. For existing context, read [Atlas and AI](../../dna/structure.md#atlas-and-the-handbook).

## E.4.2 Tech team: meeting format {#tech-team-meeting-format}

The tech team works in two-week sprints with six meetings: [sprint planning](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#tactical-sprint-planning), [governance](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#governance), and a [1:1](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#one-on-one) in week 1; a [sprint check](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#sprint-check), an [all-hands](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#all-hands), and [demo day](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md#demo-day) in week 2. [A.2.5 Tech team meeting agenda](../reference/tech-team-meeting-agenda.md) holds the steps for each meeting.

For general meeting practice, see [3.4 Facilitation](../../leadership/facilitation.md); the Fellowship's schedule there is separate from the tech team's.
