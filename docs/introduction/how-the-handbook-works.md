# 0.2 How the handbook works

## Summary

The handbook explains durable concepts and guidance; live records hold current responsibilities and work. Numbered sections help you locate a topic, and status labels show whether material is adopted, proposed, or still being drafted. Editing or publishing a page does not adopt a policy.

## 0.2.1 Chapters, sections, subsections, and appendices {#chapters-sections-and-appendices}

The handbook supports continuous training through six chapters: **0 Start here**, **1 DNA**, **2 Go deeper**, **3 Lead**, **4 Train others**, and **5 Supplemental**. Appendices A–E hold reference materials, further reading, citations, the glossary, and staff guidance. Start with DNA, then choose a deeper module, a leadership practice, or a training skill as your work calls for it.

Numbers show where a topic sits. **0** identifies this chapter; **0.2** identifies this section; **0.2.1** identifies this subsection. Appendix numbering follows the same pattern, such as **A.1.2**.

A chapter landing page summarizes the chapter and links to its sections. A section usually has its own page, with named subsections inside it. Each page has a Summary, a status, and a last-updated date. Summary and source lists are page elements, not extra numbered sections. The contents below read status directly from each page. Hover over a status symbol to see any note about unfinished material.

## 0.2.2 Beliefs, current practice, proposals, and open questions {#reading-status}

**Beliefs explain our outlook.** They describe the reasoning and values behind our work. A belief alone does not establish a process, assign authority, or approve a new requirement.

**Status shows adoption.** An adopted page describes established guidance. Draft pages are unfinished material for review; proposal pages describe possible future practice; experimental pages describe trials; reference pages provide supporting material. Planned pages are outlines for future modules, awaiting review before content is filled in. Read the page's status and any qualifications before treating an instruction as settled. The status legend in the contents also distinguishes reference material from adopted guidance: both use ✅, with their full status on hover.

**Labels show what isn't settled.** A **Proposal** block describes a possible practice, with a dashed border so it can't be mistaken for current practice. A page that is still being written shows an **Under construction** banner at the top saying what is missing; read it as a starting point, not settled guidance. Publishing a page does not change decision rights or adopt its recommendations. Some practices are already in use; others are still taking shape as we prepare for more people to join.

## 0.2.3 Where authoritative information lives {#where-authoritative-information-lives}

Information lives in three places:

| Information | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Durable definitions, expectations, and repeatable guides | The handbook, checking page status |
| Current roles, owners, governance, priorities, and work | [Atlas](https://sapiensfirst.org/atlas) or live records linked from it |
| Current policy recommendations and campaign materials | The relevant [Sapiens First website](https://sapiensfirst.org) pages, including [campaigns](https://sapiensfirst.org/campaigns) |

**The handbook explains; Atlas records.** The handbook covers our shared concepts, expectations, and practices. [Atlas](https://sapiensfirst.org/atlas) is the source of truth for current governance, work, priorities, and owners.

| The handbook tells you… | Atlas tells you… |
| --- | --- |
| What a role means | Who holds it now |
| How to plan a project | Which projects are live, and their records |
| How decisions get made | Who currently decides what |

::: source
Atlas holds current roles, circles, projects, priorities, and owners. This handbook explains the concepts, expectations, and practices behind them.
:::

For example, the handbook can explain a role; Atlas records who holds it now. An empty or unclear record does not imply that a decision has been made. Ask the relevant role holder when a current assignment or record is unclear.

## 0.2.4 How to suggest changes {#how-to-suggest-changes}

If wording is confusing or information seems outdated, tell the person responsible for that area or suggest an edit. Identify the page, explain the problem, and include a source or proposed wording when you can. Repository contributors can follow the [contributor guide](https://github.com/Sapiens-First/docs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).

Keep a factual correction or editorial change distinct from a proposed organizational decision. When suggesting a new rule, show what needs agreement and preserve its proposal status until the appropriate decision has been made.

## 0.2.5 Find anything {#find-anything}

Common questions, routed to the page, or the [Atlas](https://sapiensfirst.org/atlas) record, that answers them. The handbook explains how things work; Atlas holds who is doing what right now. Can't find your question? Try the search box or the [Glossary](../appendices/glossary.md).
