## Introduction

Since the dawn of humanity, people have used technology to build a more beautiful world.

Technology can be a remedy or a poison. It all depends on how it is used. The agricultural revolution, for example, meant less people had to labor on the fields, but also led to an increasingly feudal society. The industrial revolution increased human productivity, but also led to greater inequality. Technological innovation must be accompanied by political reformation.

This presentation aims to answer the following questions:
1. Story. Who are we as a movement? What is our story?
2. Strategy. What's our approach to creating change? What's our roadmap?
3. Culture. What culture and values do we have as a movement?
4. Structure. How are we structured, and how can you get involved?

## Part 1: Story

**Crisis**

In the digital age, we've seen many nefarious impacts of technology. Social media have allowed people to be more connected then ever, but also led to a teenage mental health crisis. Platform economies like Uber make life more inequality, but also lead to economic displacement.

Today, we are facing a great technological shift: artificial intelligence. Artificial intelligence itself is not bad or good -- it is shaped by us, its human-makers. But right now, AI is being shaped by power-hungry tech companies. 

People are worried about artificial intelligence, and rightly so. It is likely that left unchecked, AI will lead to:
- Rampant inequality. In Silicon Valley, people often refer to the "economic underclass" that will developed as AI automates vast portions of white collar work. While new jobs may eventually be created, in the meantime, vast numbers of people will be unemployed, while inequality increases.
- Threats to democracy. The power of AI is unprecedented. Money generated from the AI boom has already corrupted our political institutions. If governments and corporations use AI effectively, we could end up living in a permanent surveillance state.
- Omnicide. For decades, scientists have worried about "loss of control" of AI systems. AI itself is amoral, and often tasked with completing objectives -- even when humans are in the way. Recently, we've seen AI models use superhuman abilities to break out of sandboxes and commit real crimes. Furthermore, bad actors could easily direct AI to create deadly pandemics or take down the cyber grid.

**Vision**

But not to fear! We have faced disruptive technologies before. As a movement, Sapiens First focuses on three things:
- Life. We fight to ensure humanity survives the development of superintelligence. This means a global treaty on AI development, and international coordination to ensure advanced AI is aligned with human interests.
- Liberty. We demand a reformation to our democracy. We believe in a government for the people, by the people -- not tech oligarchs. We also believe that technology can enhance democracy by increasing civic participation, eg. by experimenting with new democratic institutions like Citizens Assemblies and liquid democracy.
- Happiness. We believe that technology should uplift humanity, not just the rich few. In practice, this means that the wealth of the AI boom should be distributed. We also believe in the reformation of governmental institutions, so that technology accelerates the development of science, public health, and economic development.

## Part 2: Strategy

Central to our DNA is a cohesive strategy & roadmap. With a clear strategy, Sapiens First chapters can be both autonomous and unified under a common goal.

**Strategy**

People typically see advocacy as falling into one of two camps:
- Structure-based organizing (eg. labor unions, typical political campaigns) use lots of one-on-one conversations and trainings to build a base.
- Mass protests get lots of people out on the streets at critical periods (eg. Black Lives Matter; Occupy Wall Street)

Successful social movements use a best-of-both-worlds approach: momentum-driven organizing. This means having solid structures & leadership development for your base, while also using "trigger events" to spur mass mobilization, and then absorbing those people into the movement.

This can be summarized in a simple diagram:
- Act. Anything from a potluck to a protest.
- Recruit. Getting connecting with participants.
- Train. Empowering recruits to self-organize new actions.

Scholars have found that successful movements win -- doing things as serious as toppling dictators -- when they get 3.5% of the population engaged in a sustain, organized manner. 

**Roadmap**

Given the fact that AI may kill us all, we focus on this as our initial primary objective. 

Mission: Federal legislation for an AI treaty
  - Phase 1: Seed cities
    - Campaigns
      - Tactics
  - Phase 2: California state
  - Phase 3: Red & blue states
  - Phase 4: 3.5%, Federal policy change

Within each Phase 1, local chapters have the freedom to organize any campaign that relates to AI safety, democracy, and abundance. We also hope chapters will experiment with tactics and share successful techniques through our shared tools.

## Part 4: Structure

To scale to the size we need, we rely on volunteers to fuel our work.

We have a Cycle of Engagement, which follows from our strategy:
- Act -> new Supporters (on the email list)
- Recruit -> new Members (pay dues + on the Discord)
- Train -> new Organizers (have an active role)

But what do Organizers do? How do we allow everyone to have a meaningful role while we scale to hundreds of thousands of people?

Sapiens First uses a system of Holacracy to allow self-organization. 

Essentially, we want people to be autonomous but also unified in our movement. This means an organizational structure that is less like a militaristic hierarchy, and more like organs in your body, or people in a city. 

This boils down to two principles:
- Rules of the road (enforceable rules)
- Clear ownership (property rights)

In Sapiens First, when you become an Organizer, you are given one or more roles. Each Role has full authority over their areas of responsibility. Roles make up Circles -- for example, the Media Circle may include a Social Media Manager, an Email Sender, and so on.
